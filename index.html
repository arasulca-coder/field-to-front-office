Context

How the league got here

1921

Fritz Pollard

Pollard co-coached the Akron Pros and became the first Black head coach in the league's history. Another Black head coach would not follow for nearly 70 years.

1934–1945

The unwritten ban

No Black players appeared in the NFL for 12 seasons under an informal agreement among owners. Kenny Washington and Woody Strode broke it with the Los Angeles Rams in 1946.

1989

Art Shell

The Raiders hired Shell, the first Black head coach of the modern era. Doug Williams had become the first Black quarterback to start and win a Super Bowl the year before.

2003

The Rooney Rule

Teams must interview candidates of color for head coach jobs, and later for GM, coordinator and senior front-office roles. It requires interviews, not hires.

2026

Ten openings, no Black hires

The 2026 hiring cycle had 10 head coach vacancies. None went to a Black coach, the fifth cycle since 2003 with no Black head coach hired. Robert Saleh was the one coach of color hired.

Today

Ownership

No NFL team has ever had a Black majority owner. Shad Khan of the Jacksonville Jaguars is the league's only majority owner of color.

About the numbers. TIDES counts players who identify as more than one race (10.9% in 2023) separately from Black players (53.5%). Press reports that count differently put the Black share of players anywhere from the high 50s to about 70%. Either way, the share falls sharply at every step from the field to the owner's box.
